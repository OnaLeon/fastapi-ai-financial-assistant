Compound Interest

Compound interest means earning interest not only on the original amount of money, but also on the interest that has already been earned. Over long periods of time, this can significantly increase the growth of savings or investments.

For example, if 10,000 euros earns 5% interest per year, after one year the balance becomes 10,500 euros. In the following year, interest is calculated on the full 10,500 euros rather than only on the original 10,000 euros.


Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses such as urgent home repairs, temporary loss of income, or unexpected travel. It is usually kept in a liquid and easily accessible account.

The purpose of an emergency fund is not to generate high investment returns. Its main purpose is financial stability and quick access to cash when unexpected expenses occur.


Diversification

Diversification means spreading investments across different assets instead of relying on a single investment. The goal is to reduce the risk that one poorly performing investment has a major impact on the entire portfolio.

Diversification can include investing across different companies, industries, countries, and asset classes such as stocks and bonds. Diversification reduces some investment risks, but it cannot eliminate all risk.


Inflation

Inflation is the general increase in prices over time. As prices rise, the purchasing power of money decreases.

For example, if inflation is 3% per year, goods costing 100 euros today may cost approximately 103 euros one year later. This means money that does not grow over time may lose purchasing power.


Investment Risk

Investments with the potential for higher returns usually also involve higher risk. The value of investments such as stocks or cryptocurrencies can rise or fall significantly.

Money that may be needed in the short term is generally exposed to different risks than money intended for long-term investment. Investment decisions should consider factors such as time horizon, liquidity needs, and the possibility of losing money.


Credit and Interest

Borrowing money usually involves paying interest to the lender. The interest rate determines part of the cost of borrowing.

A higher interest rate generally increases the total amount that must be repaid. The actual cost of a loan may also include fees and other charges in addition to the stated interest rate.
